How acids and bases work

An acid donates a proton. A base accepts one. In water, that dance decides how sour, soapy, or neutral a solution feels.

Two short definitions

In this lesson we use the Brønsted–Lowry idea, which is the one most intro classes teach first.

An acid is a molecule or ion that can donate H⁺, a bare proton. A base is a molecule or ion that can accept that proton.

In water the free proton does not float alone for long. It joins a water molecule and becomes hydronium, H₃O⁺. When people say “H⁺ in water,” they almost always mean H₃O⁺.

H⁺a proton — hydrogen’s nucleus alone
H₃O⁺hydronium — H⁺ stuck to H₂O
OH⁻hydroxide — water missing one H⁺
Neutralacid and base cancel to water and a salt

01

Acid in water · HCl

The proton handoff

Hydrogen chloride, HCl, is a strong acid. In water it gives its proton away completely. Chloride, Cl⁻, is left behind, and the water molecule that takes the proton becomes hydronium, H₃O⁺.

The free H⁺ stage is drawn briefly so you can see the handoff. In real water the proton moves along hydrogen-bond networks in femtoseconds; chemists still write H⁺ as shorthand for that motion.

Cl O H

H–Cl 1.275 Å (measured). Hydronium O–H 0.976 Å, H–O–H 111.3° (NIST CCCBDB).

02

Base in water · NaOH

Bases free hydroxide

Sodium hydroxide is an ionic solid made of Na⁺ and OH⁻. In water the ions separate. The hydroxide ion, OH⁻, is a strong base: it accepts a proton readily.

NaOH has no PubChem molecular 3D conformer because it is an ionic lattice, not a covalent molecule. The model shows the dissolved ions that matter in solution chemistry.

Na⁺ O H

OH⁻ O–H 0.964 Å (measured). Na⁺ drawn at Shannon ionic radius 1.02 Å.

03

Acid + base

They cancel to water

When hydronium meets hydroxide, one proton moves from H₃O⁺ to OH⁻. Two water molecules form. That is neutralization at the ion level:

H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2 H₂O

The leftover ions from the acid and base (for example Na⁺ and Cl⁻) stay dissolved as a salt. The water they make is neutral.

O H

H₃O⁺ meets OH⁻. After the handoff, both products are H₂O.

04

Complete vs partial transfer

Strong vs weak

A strong acid transfers its proton essentially completely in water. A weak acid transfers only a fraction of its molecules at any moment; most stay intact.

Vinegar is dilute acetic acid, CH₃COOH. About one molecule in a hundred is dissociated in a typical kitchen bottle — enough to taste sour, not enough for every molecule to have given away its proton.

Cl C O H

Strong: essentially all HCl molecules have transferred. Weak: most acetic acid molecules stay whole.

05

A count, not a calculator

What pH tells you

pH is a scale for how many hydronium ions are in the water. Lower pH means more H₃O⁺. Higher pH means fewer H₃O⁺ (and usually more OH⁻).

Pure water at about 25 °C sits near pH 7 — not because nothing is ionised, but because H₃O⁺ and OH⁻ are present in equal, tiny amounts. Lemon juice is acidic (more H₃O⁺). Soap solution is basic (more OH⁻).

Each step of 1 on the pH scale means a tenfold change in hydronium concentration. pH 3 has about ten times as many H₃O⁺ ions as pH 4.

06 · Where you meet acids and bases

In the real world

  • Nurses and pharmacistsStomach acid is mostly hydrochloric acid. Antacid tablets and liquids contain bases such as calcium carbonate or magnesium hydroxide that neutralize excess acid and ease heartburn.Acid + base → salt + water
  • Pool techniciansSwimming-pool water is kept near pH 7.2 to 7.8 so chlorine sanitizer works and swimmers’ eyes stay comfortable. Technicians add acid or base to nudge the reading.pH control
  • FarmersAcidic soil can lock up nutrients. Farmers spread agricultural lime (mostly calcium carbonate) to raise soil pH so crops can take up minerals.Liming · neutralizing soil acid
  • Janitorial workersVinegar is dilute acetic acid. Janitorial workers use it to dissolve mineral scale and clean glass because the weak acid attacks alkaline deposits without harsh fumes.Weak acid · CH₃COOH
  • Auto techniciansLead-acid car batteries hold sulfuric acid. Auto technicians wear eye and skin protection when they service batteries because concentrated acid damages tissue on contact.Strong acid · battery electrolyte
  • BakersBaking soda is sodium bicarbonate, a mild base. In dough it reacts with acidic ingredients and releases carbon dioxide gas that helps bread and cakes rise.Base in the kitchen
  • Water treatment operatorsWastewater and industrial effluent are often neutralized before discharge. Operators dose acid or base so the outflow sits near a permitted pH range that protects rivers and pipes.Neutralization at plant scale

For the classroom

Check yourself

What does an acid donate, in the Brønsted–Lowry sense?

A proton, H⁺. The acid loses that proton; a base gains it.

Why do chemists write H₃O⁺ instead of a lone H⁺ in water?

A bare proton does not float freely in water. It binds to H₂O and forms hydronium. H⁺ is still useful shorthand for that transferred proton.

Write the ion-level neutralization equation.

H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2 H₂O. The acid’s leftover anion and the base’s leftover cation remain as a dissolved salt.

Is vinegar a strong acid or a weak acid? How can you tell from the model?

Weak. Most acetic acid molecules stay intact; only a small fraction have transferred their proton at any moment. HCl, by contrast, has essentially all transferred.

Does a lower pH mean more or fewer hydronium ions?

More. Each drop of one pH unit means about ten times as many H₃O⁺ ions in the water.

Where the numbers come from

  • Measured bond lengths and angles: NIST Computational Chemistry Comparison and Benchmark Database (CCCBDB) — HCl re 1.2746 Å; H₂O O–H 0.958 Å, ∠HOH 104.48°; H₃O⁺ O–H 0.976 Å, ∠HOH 111.30°; OH⁻ O–H 0.964 Å.
